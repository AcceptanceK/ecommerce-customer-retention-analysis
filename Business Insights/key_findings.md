# US E-commerce Customer Retention — Business Insights

## Executive Summary

### Customer Retention

Of 300 registered customers, 241 had at least one delivered order, and 138 (57%) had multiple delivered orders. Another 103 received only one order, while 59 had no delivered purchases.

As of 9 November 2026, 215 customers had not purchased for over 60 days. However, using the dataset's latest delivered order date of 30 September, this falls to 150 customers (62%).

Of the 215 inactive customers, 118 were repeat buyers, making them important targets for retention campaigns.

### Marketing Performance

Email had the highest repeat purchase rate (65.4%), while Paid Search generated the most repeat customers (42).

Social Ads showed weaker customer retention in the inactivity analysis.

Customers whose first delivered order included a discount had a slightly higher repeat purchase rate (60% versus 54.8%). Further testing is needed before increasing discounts.

### Revenue Opportunities

Fashion generated the highest gross order value ($9,298) and had the lowest return rate (3.8%).

Electronics (11.7%) and Beauty (10.6%) had the highest return rates.

Of 618 orders, 94 (15%) were returned or cancelled, representing approximately $4,194 in gross order value.

The retailer should prioritise reducing returns, investigating cancellations and re-engaging inactive repeat buyers.

All values are gross, and delivered orders do not necessarily confirm payment or final revenue.

## Detailed Findings

### Finding 1: Customer Registration and Purchasing Behaviour

**Business Insight**

The retailer has 300 registered customers. Of these, 261 (87%) have placed at least one order, while 39 (13%) have never ordered.

However, these figures include cancelled and other incomplete orders. We still need to establish how many customers have successfully completed a purchase.

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should investigate when the 39 customers registered and which marketing channels brought them in.

Customers who registered some time ago but haven't ordered could receive a welcome offer or reminder to encourage their first purchase.

The retailer should also investigate order statuses to identify customers who haven't completed a successful purchase.

### Finding 2: Customer Ordering and Repeat Purchase Behaviour

**Business Insight**

Of the 261 customers who placed orders, 168 (64%) ordered more than once, while 93 (36%) ordered only once.

This suggests potential repeat-buying behaviour. However, some orders may have been cancelled, failed or unpaid, so we cannot yet confirm the actual repeat-purchase rate.

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should first check order statuses to identify customers who successfully completed multiple purchases.

For single-order customers, the retailer should investigate their last order date, products purchased and order outcomes before offering incentives to return.

Customers with completed first purchases could receive follow-up emails, personalised product recommendations or targeted discounts.

The retailer should also investigate customers with repeated failed or cancelled orders to identify possible checkout or payment problems.

### Finding 3: Order Status and Sales Performance

**Business Insight**

Of 618 orders, 506 (82%) were delivered, representing approximately $24,204 in gross order value.

Another 94 orders (15%) were cancelled or returned, representing approximately $4,194. Returns were slightly higher than cancellations.

The remaining 18 orders are still processing, with a combined gross value of approximately $957.

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should investigate why customers cancel or return orders.

For cancellations, management should examine payment issues, stock availability and delivery delays.

For returns, the retailer should identify products with high return rates and investigate possible quality, sizing or description problems.

Processing orders should also be monitored to identify delays.

Management should track cancellation and return rates monthly to measure whether improvements are working.

### Finding 4: Successful Purchases and Repeat Customers

**Business Insight**

Of the 300 registered customers, 241 have received at least one delivered order. Of these, 138 (57%) have received multiple orders, while 103 received only one.

Another 59 customers have no delivered purchases. This includes 39 who never ordered and 20 who placed orders but had none delivered.

The number of repeat customers fell from 168 to 138 when we counted delivered orders only.

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should prioritise the 20 customers who ordered but received nothing. Management should investigate cancellations, returns and processing delays before encouraging them to try again.

The 103 customers with one delivered purchase could receive follow-up emails, relevant product recommendations or targeted discounts.

For the 39 customers who never ordered, the retailer should investigate registration dates and consider welcome offers for older sign-ups.

### Finding 5: Marketing Channel Performance

**Business Insight**

Email has the highest repeat purchase rate (65.4%), followed by Social Ads (58.6%) and Organic Search (58.3%).

Paid Search brings in the most customers, with 89 registrations and 42 repeat buyers. However, its repeat purchase rate is lower at 54.5%.

Social Ads has the lowest first-purchase conversion (73%), while Referral has the lowest repeat purchase rate (53.1%).

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should investigate whether Email can attract more customers while maintaining its strong repeat purchase rate.

Paid Search should remain a priority for investigation because it generates the most repeat buyers.

Management should improve first-purchase conversion among Social Ads customers and investigate why Referral customers are less likely to return.

Before increasing marketing spending, the retailer should compare acquisition costs, customer value, order outcomes and registration dates across channels.

### Finding 6: Product Category Performance

**Business Insight**

Fashion generates the highest gross order value ($9,298) and has the lowest return rate (3.8%).

Electronics generates the second-highest value ($7,360) but has the highest return rate (11.7%), making it an important category to investigate.

Beauty also has a high return rate (10.6%) despite generating lower order value.

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should prioritise investigating Electronics returns, including product quality, compatibility and inaccurate descriptions.

Beauty should also be reviewed to understand why customers return products.

Management should investigate what contributes to Fashion's lower return rate and whether those practices can be applied to other categories.

Before increasing marketing investment, the retailer should examine product-level returns, profit margins and revenue after discounts and refunds.

### Finding 7: Discounts and Repeat Purchasing

**Business Insight**

Customers whose first delivered order included a discount had a 60% repeat purchase rate, compared with 54.8% for customers without a discount.

Both groups produced 69 repeat buyers. Although discounted customers had a slightly higher repeat purchase rate, the difference is too uncertain to conclude that discounts caused customers to return.

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should investigate further before increasing discounts.

Management should compare profit margins, discount sizes, acquisition channels and customer purchasing behaviour to understand whether discounts are financially worthwhile.

A controlled experiment could help determine whether discounts actually encourage repeat purchases.

Until then, the retailer should continue exploring other ways to encourage repeat purchases, including follow-up emails and personalised product recommendations.

### Finding 8: Customer Inactivity and Retention Risk

**Business Insight**

As of 9 November 2026, 215 of the 241 customers who previously purchased (89%) had not bought anything for over 60 days.

However, the latest delivered order in the dataset was on 30 September. Using that date instead, 150 customers (62%) were inactive.

Of the 215 inactive customers, 118 had purchased more than once, including 60 who had made at least three purchases.

Social Ads had the highest inactivity rate, while Organic Search performed better when using the September cut-off.

**Strategic Recommendation**

The retailer should prioritise contacting previously frequent buyers who haven't purchased recently.

Personalised emails, product recommendations and reminders could encourage them to return. Customers with unresolved returns or delivery problems should receive support before promotional offers.

Management should introduce 30-day and 60-day activity alerts, investigate Social Ads retention and test whether these campaigns increase repeat purchases.
